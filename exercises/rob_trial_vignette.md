# Pod 5 — Risk of bias vignette (for RoB 2 practice)

A single trial to assess with the RoB 2 tool. Read the methods, then judge each
domain as Low risk / Some concerns / High risk, and reach an overall judgement.

## Trial summary
A trial of oral zinc vs placebo for acute childhood diarrhoea in 240 children.

**Randomisation.** "Children were allocated to zinc or placebo." The method of
sequence generation is not described. Allocation concealment is not mentioned.

**Blinding / deviations.** Placebo was described as "identical in appearance".
Caregivers and outcome assessors were said to be blinded. No information on whether
any children switched treatment.

**Missing outcome data.** Of 240 randomised, outcomes are reported for 198 (82.5%).
The reasons for the 42 missing children are not given, and dropout was higher in the
placebo arm (26 vs 16).

**Measurement of the outcome.** The primary outcome (diarrhoea lasting > 7 days) was
caregiver-reported. Assessors were blinded to allocation.

**Selection of the reported result.** The paper reports the primary outcome and two
secondary outcomes. There is no reference to a pre-registered protocol or analysis plan.

## Your job
For each RoB 2 domain, decide Low / Some concerns / High and note the reason:
1. Bias arising from the randomisation process
2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions
3. Bias due to missing outcome data
4. Bias in measurement of the outcome
5. Bias in selection of the reported result

Then give an overall risk-of-bias judgement, and say what single change to the
methods (or its reporting) would most improve your confidence.

[Facilitator note: red flags are the unclear randomisation/allocation, the
differential and unexplained dropout, and the absent protocol. The blinding and
identical placebo are reassuring. A defensible overall judgement is 'Some concerns'
tending to 'High' because of the differential missing data.]
